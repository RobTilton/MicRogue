# ToolShed
Updated: 2026-10-03

## Purpose And Ownership

ToolShed preserves tools, reusable components and proven rules with a strong likelihood of helping Rob's future workflows. A tool earns its place through an identifiable repeated operation, useful human access point, or avoided rediscovery cost. A small proven coordinate or transform rule can be worth preserving alongside a complete authoring tool.

Active game subsystems belong with their approved Production owners once validated and adopted. Modularity or reusable code alone does not make an active runtime subsystem a ToolShed tool. A catalog-entry assistant may live here while the runtime catalog and consumers remain owned by their Production system.

## Discovery And Reuse

The [parts catalog](Ledger/ToolShed_Parts_Catalog.md) discovers individual capabilities; the [build catalog](Ledger/Completed_Tool_Builds_Catalog.md) discovers complete authoring workflows. Keep entries concise and searchable by object, operation, API, problem and Rob's common phrasing. Read the relevant owner's contract before using or rebuilding a capability.

[StorageUnits](StorageUnits/README.md) holds reusable parts/rules. [Completed_Tool_Builds](Completed_Tool_Builds/README.md) holds composed tools. Follow [ToolShed Format](Ledger/ToolShed_Format.md). Each owner explains what the tool does, inputs/outputs, entry point, assumptions, mutation boundary, evidence and failure limits. Identify the human-side scene or control that lets Rob point directly at the material being discussed. References must resolve to current owners; runtime data remains authoritative with its runtime owner.

## Preservation And Retirement

Assess task-built tools for likely future reuse against Rob's established workflows before retiring them. Preserve useful tools and their contracts rather than treating them as disposable task output. One-off helpers need not become generalized infrastructure merely because they exist. Prototype and validate in a bounded Room; keep proven capabilities discoverable without retaining unrelated development history in their contracts.

Material cleared for human sorting moves to Workshop/Trashcans after required outputs have verified durable owners and consumers no longer depend on the retiring paths. Routine movement of already-designated sorting material does not require repeated permission. Rob owns sorting, off-site storage and garbage collection. Trash contents are excluded from Godot discovery and Git through local exclusion files; neither retirement nor ToolShed placement authorizes deletion, commits or Production adoption.
